NHANES Hypertension Analysis Report
1. Objective

The objective of this analysis was to estimate the prevalence of hypertension among U.S. adults using NHANES 2021–2023 data and examine demographic and cardiometabolic factors associated with hypertension while accounting for the complex NHANES survey design.

2. Data Source and Study Population

Data were obtained from the 2021–2023 National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES). Demographic, body measurement, blood pressure examination, blood pressure questionnaire, diabetes, physical activity, and smoking datasets were linked using the unique participant identifier (SEQN).

The analysis was restricted to adults aged 20 years or older (n = 7,809).

3. Outcome Definition

Hypertension was defined using measured blood pressure and antihypertensive medication use. Participants were classified as having hypertension if they had:

mean systolic blood pressure ≥130 mmHg, mean diastolic blood pressure ≥80 mmHg, or current use of antihypertensive medication.

Mean systolic and diastolic blood pressures were calculated from available oscillometric measurements.

4. Covariates

The analysis considered age, sex, race/ethnicity, educational attainment, BMI category, diabetes status, and smoking status.

5. Statistical Analysis

The analysis accounted for the complex NHANES sampling design using examination weights, strata, and primary sampling units.

Survey-weighted hypertension prevalence and 95% confidence intervals were estimated overall and across demographic and clinical subgroups.

The primary adjusted model included age group, sex, BMI category, and diabetes status.

Because hypertension was common, survey-weighted Poisson regression with a log link was used to estimate adjusted prevalence ratios (aPRs) and 95% confidence intervals.

6. Results

Hypertension prevalence increased substantially with age:

20–39 years: 24.9%
40–59 years: 52.8%
≥60 years: 72.0%

Prevalence was 45.9% among females and 52.7% among males.

Among racial/ethnic groups, non-Hispanic Black adults had the highest observed unadjusted prevalence (60.9%). These racial/ethnic estimates were descriptive and unadjusted.

Hypertension prevalence also increased across BMI categories:

Normal weight: 33.5%
Overweight: 44.6%
Obesity: 63.7%

Hypertension prevalence was 76.5% among adults with diabetes compared with 45.5% among adults without diabetes.

Adjusted Analysis

After adjustment for age group, sex, BMI category, and diabetes:

Adults aged 40–59 had 1.96 times the prevalence of hypertension compared with adults aged 20–39 (aPR 1.96; 95% CI: 1.76–2.18).

Adults aged ≥60 had 2.74 times the prevalence of hypertension compared with adults aged 20–39 (aPR 2.74; 95% CI: 2.40–3.12).

Males had 17% higher hypertension prevalence than females (aPR 1.17; 95% CI: 1.11–1.23).

Adults with obesity had 74% higher hypertension prevalence than adults with normal BMI (aPR 1.74; 95% CI: 1.57–1.94).

Adults with diabetes had 17% higher hypertension prevalence than adults without diabetes (aPR 1.17; 95% CI: 1.12–1.23).

7. Interpretation

The main finding is that hypertension prevalence increased markedly with age and BMI.

After adjustment, older age showed the largest association with hypertension prevalence, followed by obesity. Male sex and diabetes were also associated with higher hypertension prevalence.

Because NHANES is cross-sectional, these findings represent associations and should not be interpreted as causal relationships.
